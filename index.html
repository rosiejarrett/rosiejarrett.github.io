Why this room, this week

Africa’s borders cut across communities, ecosystems, markets and transport networks. A country can hold strong national preparedness and remain exposed if its border districts are disconnected.

Collaboration during Ebola, Marburg and mpox has been real but event-based: agreements signed in an emergency, stood down once it passes. AI, machine learning and advanced analytics can turn that reactive posture into preparedness intelligence: anticipating threats, forecasting impact and directing investment before a local event becomes a regional emergency.

Three dialogues

Ninety minutes, three conversations: political trust, responsible AI, and the money that has to move upstream.

Leadership

Political trust across borders

What agreements let countries share preparedness information fast? How do border communities become the first line of regional preparedness rather than the last point of national surveillance?

Innovation

From data to intelligence to decisions

Where does AI deliver value now? How are epidemiological, climate, mobility, genomic and animal-health data combined responsibly, and how does Africa avoid dependence on externally controlled algorithms?

Partnership

Financing intelligence before emergencies

How development banks, donors, philanthropy and the private sector move investment upstream, from financing response to financing anticipation and readiness.

The goal is not one centralised database but a federated, trusted ecosystem: countries keep appropriate control of their data while the region generates the intelligence collective security requires.
